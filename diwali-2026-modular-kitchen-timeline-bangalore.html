Want Your Modular Kitchen or Wardrobe Ready Before Diwali 2026? Here's the Timeline That Actually Works

Diwali 2026 falls on November 8. A week-by-week backward-planning guide to when you actually need to sign off, by scope, not by hope.

Wood & Co. Interiors · September 2026 · 7 min read

Every September, the same phone call happens. A homeowner tells us they want the kitchen "sorted before Diwali," sometimes with three weeks to go, occasionally with three days. Diwali 2026 falls on Sunday, November 8, which means today, there are exactly nine weeks left. That sounds like plenty of time. For a coat of paint, it is. For a proper modular kitchen or wardrobe, it's already tight, and for some scopes, it's too late.

This isn't a sales pitch dressed up as a deadline. It's the actual backward math: how long each stage of a real modular project takes, and what date you need to have signed off by, by scope, so you know today, not in October, whether Diwali is realistic for your home.

Why the Diwali deadline catches people off guard

Most of the confusion comes from comparing modular furniture to showroom furniture. A sofa or dining table is made, sitting in a warehouse, and can be delivered in days. A modular kitchen or wardrobe is manufactured only after your site is measured and your design is frozen. Nothing exists until you approve it. That single fact is what most "quick Diwali makeover" articles skip, because they're written about painting and civil touch-ups, not modular carcass-and-shutter work.

The second thing people underestimate is the seasonal squeeze. Factories producing carcass panels, shutters, and edge banding across Bangalore run at or near full capacity from September through November every year, because every interior studio in the city is fielding the same Diwali request at once.

What Festive-Season Backlog Actually Looks Like

Hettich and other hardware brands see stock movement tighten in this window as festive demand rises nationally. Booking early isn't just about getting an installation slot; it's about getting the finish and hardware you actually want, rather than whatever happens to be in stock in late October.

The real process, stage by stage

Here's what actually has to happen between "I want a new kitchen" and cooking your first festive meal in it:

  1. Site visit & measurement: 1 to 3 days to schedule, done on-site.
  2. Design & BOQ finalisation: layout, material selection (Greenply BWP/BWR carcass, HDHMR shutters where specified), and a line-item Bill of Quantities. Typically 4 to 10 days depending on how many rounds of revision you need.
  3. Advance payment & order confirmation: our schedule runs 50 percent advance, 40 percent on delivery, 10 percent on handover. Manufacturing only starts once the advance clears.
  4. Manufacturing: 3 to 4 weeks in a normal month, closer to 4 weeks in the September to November festive window because factories are booked out further in advance.
  5. Site installation: 3 to 7 days for a single kitchen or wardrobe, longer for multi-room scopes, and dependent on your site being genuinely ready.
  6. Snag list & handover: 1 to 3 days to fix minor fit and finish issues before the balance payment and final handover.

Add it up on a normal timeline and a single modular kitchen, start to finish, is a 5-to-6-week commitment even when nothing goes wrong. That's the number every Diwali deadline in this guide is built around.

One Condition Sits Above Everything Else

Every timeline below assumes your site is civil-ready (electrical points finalised, plumbing done for the sink, flooring laid and dust-free) on the day manufacturing finishes. If civil work is still pending, none of these dates apply; the modular clock hasn't started yet.

If you're starting today, here's what still fits

Nine weeks is enough for a genuinely good kitchen or wardrobe, but only with a few trade-offs most people don't think to make in September:

  • Choose from standard finishes and laminates rather than imported or custom veneer, which can add 2 to 3 weeks of import and procurement time on its own.
  • Freeze the design in the first meeting or two. Every extra round of "let me think about the handle colour" costs days you don't have in this window.
  • Confirm the advance within 48 hours of receiving the quotation. Manufacturing slots in this season fill up in the order payments clear, not the order enquiries come in.
  • Make sure civil work is actually finished, not "almost finished." A kitchen carcass can't go in around wet paint or an unfinished electrical point.

Questions worth asking before you sign anything

If you're evaluating more than one interior partner for a Diwali-timed project, the quotation total tells you very little. These four questions tell you whether the date on the contract is real:

  • "Which factory is manufacturing this, and what's their current backlog?" A vague answer here usually means your order gets slotted in whenever there's spare capacity, not necessarily in the order you signed.
  • "Is this finish and hardware in stock, or on order?" Anything on order during festive season can add 1 to 3 weeks with no warning, and that delay rarely shows up in the original contract.
  • "What happens if my civil work runs late?" A partner who has thought this through will give you a real contingency, not just a shrug, because a delayed site pushes everything else back regardless of how fast manufacturing goes.
  • "What's excluded from this quote?" Appliances, chimney ducting, false ceiling, and GST are commonly left out of a first estimate. Knowing this before you sign avoids a second, unplanned round of approvals eating into your remaining weeks.

None of these questions are awkward to ask, and a studio that has genuinely planned its festive-season capacity will answer all four in a couple of minutes. Hesitation on any of them is worth treating as a scheduling risk, not just a communication gap.

Frequently asked questions

Can a carpenter really finish a modular kitchen in two weeks before Diwali?

Only for a very small, stock-material job on a site that's already fully civil-ready. A proper modular kitchen, with a factory-manufactured carcass, shutters, hardware, and a professional site fit, realistically needs 5 to 6 weeks minimum from sign-off to handover, before the festive-season backlog is even factored in.

What if my site still has pending civil work?

Then the modular timeline hasn't started yet. Electrical points, sink plumbing, and flooring need to be complete and dust-free before installation, so any pending civil work pushes every deadline in this guide back by however long that work takes.

Does rushing an order for Diwali cost more?

Indirectly, yes. Rush timelines mean fewer material and finish choices, since you're limited to whatever is already in stock, and factories running at festive-season capacity have far less flexibility to negotiate on price or delivery dates.

What should I prioritise if I can't finish everything before Diwali?

The kitchen and main living area, since those are the spaces guests actually see and use during the festival. Bedroom wardrobes and secondary rooms can usually wait a few more weeks without anyone noticing.

The bottom line

Diwali is a genuinely good reason to finally get the kitchen or wardrobe you've been putting off, but it's a deadline the modular process doesn't bend for just because a date matters emotionally. The honest version of this guide is short: if your scope is a full home, that window has already closed for this year, and early 2027 is the more realistic target. If it's a single kitchen or a set of wardrobes, you still have a real shot, provided the design gets frozen quickly and the site is genuinely ready. And if it's smaller than that (hardware, a unit facelift, paint), you have far more breathing room than the panic suggests.

The same math applies whether you're in Bangalore or at our Kulur (Mangalore) studio, though Mangalore's smaller pool of fabrication vendors can mean slightly tighter lead times on custom hardware during this exact season, worth flagging early if that's where your project is.
